## When to use

Reporting on what a campaign or a channel actually did, for people who will make budget decisions from it.

## Report against the target that was set

Open with the outcome number, the target, and the variance. Everything else is explanation. A report that leads with impressions when the target was pipeline is answering a question nobody asked.

## Show the funnel, not the endpoints

Volume at each stage with the conversion rate between them, against the prior period. The stage where the rate moved is the finding - a campaign that delivered twice the clicks and the same conversions has a landing page problem, and only the funnel view shows that.

## Be honest about attribution

State the model - last touch, first touch, or something else - and what it over-credits. Last touch flatters the bottom of the funnel; first touch flatters the top. If there was a holdout, lead with the incremental number, because it is the only one that answers "was this worth doing".

## Gotchas

- Compare like periods. Four weeks against a five-week month is a fabricated trend.
- Sample size: do not report a conversion-rate change on a denominator in the tens.
- Report cost per outcome, not spend. Spend alone cannot be judged.
- Say what you would do differently. A report with no recommendation makes the reader do the analysis again.
