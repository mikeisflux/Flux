## When to use

Writing outreach that gets a reply, at more than one-at-a-time volume, without it reading as generated.

## The structure that works

Four sentences, in this order:

1. **The specific reason for writing** - the trigger, named, with enough detail that it could only be about them.
2. **The problem you believe that creates**, stated as a hypothesis and not as a fact about their business.
3. **One sentence of evidence** you can help - a comparable customer, a number.
4. **A small ask.** "Worth fifteen minutes?" outperforms a calendar link in a first email.

## Personalisation that is worth the time

Real personalisation refers to something that took effort to find and that matters to them: a change in their job postings, a specific line in their pricing page, a talk they gave. Mentioning their city, their industry, or that you "came across their profile" is not personalisation and reads worse than being plainly generic.

## Write it to be read on a phone

Under 120 words. No preamble. Subject line lowercase and specific, three to five words. One link at most. No attachment on a first email.

## Gotchas

- Never claim a mutual connection, a prior conversation, or a shared event that did not happen.
- Do not send anything you would be embarrassed to have quoted back to you in public.
- A draft is a draft: outreach that could actually reach a person needs a human to press send, and nothing here should send on its own.
