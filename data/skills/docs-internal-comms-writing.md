## When to use

Writing something the whole company, or a large part of it, has to read and act on.

## Put the news in the first sentence

People skim internal email. Whatever the announcement is, it is the first line - not the third paragraph after the context. If the news is bad, that is doubly true: burying it reads as an attempt to bury it, and that is what the message will be remembered for.

## Answer the reader's actual question

For every internal announcement the reader is asking one thing: **what does this mean for me?** Answer it explicitly, by audience if the answer differs. If the answer is "nothing changes for you", say that - it is the most reassuring sentence available and it is almost always omitted.

## Structure

1. The news, one sentence.
2. What changes, and for whom, and when.
3. Why - briefly, and honestly.
4. What you need people to do, with a date.
5. Where to ask questions, and who answers them.

## Gotchas

- Do not use a euphemism for something serious. Everyone decodes it and then distrusts the rest.
- Say what is not yet decided rather than leaving a silence; silence gets filled with a worse story.
- One message, one topic. A reorganisation and a policy change in the same email means one of them is not read.
- Time the send: nothing significant late on a Friday.
