## When to use

Preparing the quarterly review of performance, plans and problems.

## Structure it as an argument, not a report

1. **What we said we would do** last quarter.
2. **What happened**, against each commitment - including what did not.
3. **Why**, for the material gaps.
4. **What we are doing about it.**
5. **What we commit to next quarter**, specifically enough to be judged the same way.

A QBR that only reports results without referencing the prior commitments cannot be held to account, which is usually why it is structured that way.

## Be specific about misses

The misses are where the value is. For each: what happened, whether it was a plan problem or an execution problem, what was learned, and what changes. A deck that explains every miss as an external factor is not a review.

## Bring the decisions you need

A QBR with no asks is a status update. List the decisions required, with the options and a recommendation for each, and put them where they will get time rather than at the end.

## Gotchas

- Send the material in advance and use the meeting for discussion. Reading slides aloud wastes the most expensive hour in the quarter.
- Use the same metrics as last quarter, defined the same way.
- Keep the appendix separate; a 60-slide deck with 12 slides of argument reads as evasion.
