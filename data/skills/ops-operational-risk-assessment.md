## When to use

Identifying what could go wrong in an operation and deciding what to do about it.

## Enumerate by category, so nothing is forgotten

People (key person, error, fraud), process (failure, gap, control weakness), systems (outage, data loss, security), external (supplier, regulatory, physical), and financial (liquidity, credit, currency). Working through categories finds risks that free association does not.

## Score likelihood and impact separately, on a defined scale

Write the scale down before scoring - what "likely" means in frequency, what each impact band means in currency or in hours of disruption. Undefined scales produce scores that cannot be compared between assessors or over time.

## Decide a treatment for every risk

Avoid, reduce, transfer, or accept. Each with an owner and a date. **Accept** is a legitimate treatment and must be recorded as an explicit decision by someone with the authority to make it - an unrecorded acceptance is just an unmanaged risk.

## Test the controls you are relying on

A risk scored low because a control mitigates it is only low if the control actually works. Where the residual score depends on a control, note when it was last tested. Untested controls are the most common way a risk register is wrong.

## Gotchas

- Single points of failure - one person, one supplier, one system - are systematically under-scored because they have not failed yet.
- Review after every incident, whether or not the incident was on the register. What is missing is the finding.
- A register nobody revisits is a document, not a control.
