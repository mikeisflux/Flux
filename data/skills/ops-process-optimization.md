## When to use

Making an existing process faster or cheaper without breaking what it is for.

## Measure it before changing it

Map the current process step by step with, for each: who does it, how long it takes, how long it waits before the next step, and how often it is reworked. Wait time between steps is usually the majority of elapsed time and is almost never where people look.

## Attack in this order

1. **Eliminate** - steps that produce nothing anyone uses. Every process accumulates these, usually as a response to an incident that nobody remembers.
2. **Reduce waiting** - batching, handoffs and approvals. Cheaper to fix than the work itself and usually a larger share of the elapsed time.
3. **Simplify** the remaining steps.
4. **Automate** last. Automating a step that should not exist makes it permanent.

## Understand why a control exists before removing it

Every approval and every check was added by someone in response to something. Find out what before removing it. Some are obsolete; some are the only thing preventing an expensive failure, and the difference is not visible from the process map.

## Gotchas

- Measure after the change against the same definition, and include quality and rework, not just speed. Faster with more errors is not an improvement.
- Change one thing at a time or you cannot attribute the effect.
- The people doing the work know where the waste is. Ask them first; it is the cheapest research available.
