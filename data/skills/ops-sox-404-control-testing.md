## When to use

Testing whether an internal control over financial reporting actually operated, to a standard that will survive review.

## Understand the control before testing it

Write down: what risk it addresses, who performs it, how often, what evidence it produces, and what would constitute a failure. A test designed without this tests whatever the evidence happens to show rather than whether the control worked.

## Distinguish design from operating effectiveness

**Design** - would this control, performed as described, prevent or detect the risk? Assess before testing. A well-operated control with a design gap is still a deficiency, and testing operation first wastes the effort.

**Operation** - did it actually happen, every time, for the period?

## Test with evidence, not enquiry

Enquiry alone is not evidence. Rank the methods: re-performance is strongest, then inspection of evidence, then observation, then enquiry. For each item tested record what was inspected, the date, who performed the control, and the conclusion.

## Conclude precisely

Every exception documented with the specific item, what was expected and what was found. Then evaluate: is it an isolated instance or a control failure? Extrapolate the error rate before concluding, rather than after being asked to.

## Gotchas

- An IT-dependent control needs the underlying report tested for completeness and accuracy too, or the test is of a number nobody validated.
- A control performed but not evidenced is a control that failed for testing purposes. Say so.
- Do not test a control that has changed mid-period as one control; it is two populations.
