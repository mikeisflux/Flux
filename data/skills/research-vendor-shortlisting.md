## When to use

Narrowing a field of vendors to two or three worth evaluating properly.

## Write the requirements before looking

Split into must-have and nice-to-have, and be honest about which is which. A requirement list written after seeing the options is a list that describes the favourite. Include the non-functional ones - data residency, SSO, uptime commitment, support hours, exit terms - because those are what disqualify late and expensively.

## Disqualify fast, on the cheap criteria

Run the whole field against the must-haves first, using public information only. Anything failing a must-have is out, with the reason recorded. This should remove most of the field in an hour and is the highest-value step in the process.

## Compare the survivors on the same grid

One table, same criteria, same evidence standard. For each cell: the finding and where it came from. Where a vendor's answer is "yes with conditions", record the conditions - that is where the difference usually is.

## Cost the whole thing

Licence, implementation, integration effort, training, and what it costs to leave. A cheaper licence with a heavy integration and a proprietary data format is often the expensive option.

## Gotchas

- A reference the vendor supplies is a reference they chose. Ask for one at your scale and in your sector, and ask them what went wrong.
- Check the security documentation exists before shortlisting; discovering it does not at contract stage costs a month.
- Record why each rejected vendor was rejected. That list is what stops the process restarting when someone new joins the discussion.
