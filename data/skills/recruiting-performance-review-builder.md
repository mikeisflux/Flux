## When to use

Preparing a performance review that is specific, evidenced and useful to the person receiving it.

## Gather the evidence before forming the view

Doing it the other way round produces a narrative and then finds support for it. Collect first: shipped work and its outcomes, peer input, the goals set last cycle and what happened to them, and specific incidents both ways with dates.

## Write against the expectations for the level

Every judgement anchored to the written expectation for their level, not to a comparison with a colleague and not to a general impression. Where there is no written expectation, say so - that is a finding about the framework, and the review should not silently invent one.

## Be specific, and balanced in specificity

The most common failure is precise criticism and vague praise, which reads as criticism throughout. If a concern gets a dated example, so does a strength. Two or three of each is enough; a list of twelve is not read.

## Make the development section actionable

One or two areas, each with what changes, what support is available, and how it will be visible next cycle. "Be more strategic" is not feedback; "own the design for one project end to end, including the stakeholder alignment" is.

## Gotchas

- Recency bias is the dominant distortion. Deliberately review the whole period, especially the first half.
- Nothing in a written review should be a surprise. If it is, that is a management failure to raise it at the time, and it is worth saying so.
- Separate performance from potential and from compensation. Conflating them makes all three conversations worse.
