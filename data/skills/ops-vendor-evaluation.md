## When to use

Evaluating a shortlisted vendor properly, before committing.

## Test against your own case

A scripted demo proves the vendor can drive their own product. Give them your actual scenario, with your data shape and your volume, and watch them do it. Most of what a shortlist misses shows up here: the workflow that needs three tools, the field that does not exist, the export that is not really an export.

## Evaluate the things that outlast the features

- **Support** - hours, response commitment, and what happens at severity one. Ask what their actual median response was last quarter.
- **Roadmap** - and how much of what you need is on it rather than in it.
- **Security and compliance** - the documentation, not the assertion.
- **Data** - who owns it, how you get it out, in what format, and what it costs.
- **Exit** - notice, assistance, and what happens to the data. Negotiate this at the start, when you have leverage.

## Talk to a reference you found yourself

Vendor-supplied references are selected. Find a customer of similar size in a similar sector independently and ask them what went wrong and what they wish they had known. That conversation is worth more than the rest of the evaluation.

## Gotchas

- Cost the integration and the internal effort, not just the licence.
- Check the contract for unilateral price or terms changes before falling in love with the product.
- Score against the criteria written before the demos. Criteria adjusted afterwards describe the winner.
