## When to use

Working out what to pay for a role, or whether current pay is defensible.

## Fix the comparison before looking at numbers

A benchmark is meaningless without: the level, the location or the pay geography, the company size and stage, and the industry. Most disagreements about "market rate" are two people quoting different comparison sets.

## Use more than one source and reconcile them

Survey data, offers you have actually made and lost, and what candidates report. They will disagree; the disagreement is informative. Recent lost offers are the most current signal available and the most under-weighted.

## Compare total compensation, not base

Base, bonus at target, equity with its assumed value and vesting, pension, and anything material in benefits. An equity number without the valuation assumption stated is not a number. Compare like for like or the analysis is decorative.

## Then check internal equity

Before recommending an external number, look inside: does it create an inversion against someone at the same level with more tenure? An offer that fixes a hiring problem and creates three retention problems is a bad trade, and it is the one most often made under time pressure.

## Gotchas

- Survey data lags by six to twelve months in a moving market.
- Self-reported public data over-represents the top of the range.
- Pay transparency legislation may require a published range; check before publishing one that does not match the internal band.
- Document the reasoning. A compensation decision without a written rationale gets relitigated every cycle.
