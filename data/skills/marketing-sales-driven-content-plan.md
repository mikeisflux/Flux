## When to use

Deciding what content to make based on what sales conversations actually get stuck on.

## Get the evidence from the calls, not from a keyword tool

The material is already there: recorded calls, lost-deal reasons, the questions in the inbox, the objections in the CRM. Pull the recurring ones and count them. Frequency of a question is the ranking, and it is a far better ranking than search volume for anything below the top of the funnel.

## Sort by where in the deal they occur

- **Early** - "what is this and why would I care" - becomes public, discoverable content.
- **Middle** - "how does it compare to X", "will it work with our stack" - becomes comparison pages and technical documentation, and is the most under-produced category.
- **Late** - security, procurement, migration - becomes a one-pager the rep sends. Rarely public, very high value per piece.

## Make the piece the rep will actually send

The test of a sales-driven piece is whether a rep sends it unprompted. That means it answers one objection completely, it is a page not a chapter, and it is written to be forwarded to someone who was not on the call.

## Gotchas

- Ask reps what they currently send instead. The homemade deck someone built in a hurry is the highest-signal brief available.
- Re-check quarterly: objections change as the product and the market change, and the plan should not be annual.
- Track which pieces get sent. The ones that never do should be retired, not rewritten.
