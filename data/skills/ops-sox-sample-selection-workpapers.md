## When to use

Selecting a sample for control testing and documenting it so someone else could reach the same conclusion.

## Define the population first, and prove it is complete

The most common workpaper failure is not the sample but the population it came from. Establish the source, the date range, the record count, and evidence that the list is complete - a reconciliation to a system total, not an assertion. An incomplete population invalidates everything downstream.

## Size the sample by frequency and risk

Standard guidance ties sample size to how often the control operates - a handful for a monthly control, more for a daily one, and higher for higher risk. Record the basis for the size chosen rather than the number alone.

## Select in a way that can be re-performed

Random with a documented seed, or systematic with a documented interval and start point. Either way, someone else must be able to reproduce the exact selection. Haphazard selection is not random and does not survive review.

## Write the workpaper to stand alone

Purpose, population and completeness evidence, sample method and size with rationale, the items selected, the test performed on each, the results, the exceptions, and the conclusion. A reviewer should need nothing else and no conversation.

## Gotchas

- If an exception is found, do not quietly extend the sample. Document the exception, then document the decision to extend and why.
- Cross-reference every assertion to its supporting document.
- Sign and date. An unsigned workpaper is not evidence of who did the work.
