## When to use

Writing a reply to a customer that resolves the thing rather than continuing the thread.

## Answer the question in the first line

Yes, no, or here is how. Then the detail. A reply that opens with context makes the customer read three paragraphs to find out whether they got what they asked for, and they will reply asking.

## Answer everything they asked

Multi-question emails are routinely answered on the first question only, which guarantees another round trip. Enumerate their questions and answer each, in their order.

## Match register, not length

Mirror their formality and their terminology. Do not mirror their frustration. Keep it short - a long reply to a short question reads as evasion, and a long reply to an angry customer reads as a defence.

## Be accurate about what is not true

- If it does not work, say it does not work.
- If it is planned, say planned, never soon, and never with a date that has not been committed.
- If you do not know, say you will find out and give a time.

The most damaging replies are the confidently approximate ones.

## Gotchas

- A draft is a draft. Anything going to a customer gets a human's send.
- Never promise a fix date on someone else's behalf.
- Check the thread history before replying; contradicting a colleague's earlier answer is worse than the original problem.
