## When to use

A call is booked and there are fifteen minutes to get ready. The output is a page you can hold in front of you, not a research report.

## Build the page

One side of A4, in this order:

1. **Who is on the call** - name, role, tenure, and one thing about them that is real.
2. **What the company does**, in a sentence a customer would recognise, plus recent news: funding, launches, leadership changes, layoffs.
3. **History with us** - every prior touch, what was said, what was promised. Nothing loses a deal faster than asking a question the last call answered.
4. **The likely reason they took the call** - inferred from the inbound path, the role, or the trigger event.
5. **Three questions** you actually want answered.
6. **The one objection** most likely to come up, and the answer.

## Where to look

CRM first for history, then the company's own site for how they describe themselves, then news and filings for what changed, then the attendees' profiles for tenure and remit. Job postings are the most underused source: they say what a company is building before the website does.

## Gotchas

- Do not put anything on the page you cannot say out loud to the person. Research that sounds like surveillance loses the room.
- A trigger event more than a quarter old is not a trigger.
- If the history is thin, say so on the page rather than padding it. Knowing you have no history is useful.
