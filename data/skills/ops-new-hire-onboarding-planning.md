## When to use

Planning someone's first weeks so they become productive rather than politely lost.

## Get the mechanics done before day one

Accounts, hardware, access to the systems they need, calendar invitations, and their name in the places it should appear. Every hour of day one spent waiting for an account is an hour that signals how organised the place is, and first impressions of that are durable.

## Plan to a first contribution, not to a schedule of meetings

Pick something small, real and shippable in the first week or two, and make sure the path to it is clear. Nothing establishes belonging faster than having done something. A first month of reading documentation and meeting people produces someone who feels like a visitor.

## Structure the first month

- **Week 1** - context, tools, one small task, meeting the immediate team.
- **Week 2-3** - a real piece of work with support, and the wider set of people.
- **Week 4** - working normally, and a check-in on what was confusing.

Name a buddy separate from the manager for the questions people do not want to ask their manager.

## Gotchas

- Write down what "good" looks like at 30, 60 and 90 days, and share it with them on day one. Unstated expectations are the most common cause of an early departure.
- Collect what was confusing at the end of month one and fix it for the next person; the newest person is the only one who can see the gaps.
- Do not schedule the whole first week. Someone with no unstructured time cannot absorb anything.
