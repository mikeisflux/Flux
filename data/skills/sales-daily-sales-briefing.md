## When to use

The first ten minutes of the day: what changed overnight and what needs action before lunch.

## What goes in

In priority order, and nothing else:

1. **Replies waiting on you**, oldest first, with the thread's last line quoted.
2. **Deals with a date this week** - a close date, a renewal, a contract expiry.
3. **Deals that went quiet** past their normal cadence, with days since last contact.
4. **Today's meetings**, each with the one fact you need going in.
5. **Account signals overnight** - a news item, a product usage change, a support escalation on an open deal.

## Rules that keep it useful

- Cap it. A briefing longer than one screen gets skimmed and then ignored.
- Every item has a next action, or it is information and belongs at the bottom.
- Sort by what decays fastest, not by deal size.
- If nothing needs action, say so. A briefing that manufactures urgency daily stops being read.

## Gotchas

- Timezones: "overnight" is different for a rep in a different region. Anchor to the reader's local day.
- A deal is not quiet because the CRM was not updated. Check the inbox before flagging silence.
- Do not include forecast maths here. That is a weekly exercise and it distracts from the calls.
