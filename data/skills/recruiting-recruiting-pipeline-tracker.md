## When to use

Keeping a hiring pipeline in a state where you can tell whether it is going to work.

## Track the funnel by stage, per role

Applied, screened, first interview, onsite, offer, accepted. Conversion between each. The point is not the totals - it is finding the stage where the rate is unusually low, because that is where the process is broken:

- Low apply-to-screen: the sourcing or the job description.
- Low screen-to-interview: the screen is not selective enough, or the bar is not shared.
- Low onsite-to-offer: the loop is not calibrated, or the role is misspecified.
- Low offer-to-accept: compensation, or the candidate experience during the loop.

## Track time, not just counts

Days in each stage, per candidate. Time in stage is the metric candidates feel, and the best candidates are the ones with other options and the least patience. A candidate sitting six days awaiting scheduling is a candidate being lost quietly.

## Keep the reasons

Rejection reason at every stage, from a fixed list. This is the only data that tells you whether you are rejecting for the right things, and it is the first field to be left blank under pressure.

## Gotchas

- Candidate data is personal data. Retention limits apply, and the tracker is not a place for informal commentary.
- Pipeline totals without a conversion history cannot forecast anything.
- A stalled candidate is not a slow candidate. Chase or close them; leaving them open flatters the pipeline.
