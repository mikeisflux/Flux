## When to use

An inquiry arrives with legal weight - a complaint, a demand, a regulator's letter - and the first decision is what to do with it.

## Identify what it is and what clock it started

Many inquiries carry a statutory or contractual deadline, and it usually runs from receipt rather than from when someone noticed. Establish the type, the sender's standing, the deadline, and the consequence of missing it, before anything else.

## Escalate by category, immediately

Escalate on sight, without attempting a response:

- Anything from a regulator, a court, or a law enforcement body.
- A letter before action, or any claim of infringement.
- A data subject request, a breach notification, or anything naming data protection law.
- Anything alleging discrimination, harassment or safety.
- Anything threatening publicity.

For these the only correct first action is acknowledgement of receipt and routing to counsel.

## For the rest, respond carefully

Acknowledge promptly. Answer only what was asked. Do not speculate, do not apologise in terms that admit liability, and do not volunteer information. Have a second person read it before it is sent.

## Gotchas

- Preserve everything the moment an inquiry arrives, including messages and drafts. Deletion after notice is a far worse problem than the original matter.
- Do not forward externally without checking privilege.
- Record the date and channel of receipt. Disputes about deadlines are disputes about that date.
