## When to use

Planning a campaign before anything is produced, so that it can be judged afterwards.

## Define the measurable outcome first

One number, with a baseline and a target date. "Awareness" is not one. If the outcome cannot be measured with the instrumentation that exists today, either fix the instrumentation as part of the campaign or pick a different outcome - a campaign you cannot evaluate teaches nothing.

## Then work backwards through the funnel

From the target outcome, back out the volume needed at each preceding stage using current conversion rates. This is the step that turns an ambition into a plan, and it is the step that most often reveals the target is impossible with the budget.

## Plan the pieces around one message

- **The message** - one sentence, the thing you want remembered.
- **The audience segments**, and what differs for each.
- **The channels**, chosen because the audience is there rather than because they are available.
- **The assets** each channel needs, with owners and dates.
- **The landing experience** - which is usually the weakest link and the cheapest to fix.

## Decide the kill criteria

Before launch, write down what result at what point would mean stopping. Campaigns without a stop condition run to their end date regardless of performance, spending the tail of the budget on something already known not to work.

## Gotchas

- Check tracking end to end before spending anything. A campaign with broken attribution is a campaign with no result.
- Build in a holdout where the channel allows it, or the measurement is a correlation.
- Timing against known events - holidays, competitor launches, your own releases - is worth more than most creative decisions.
