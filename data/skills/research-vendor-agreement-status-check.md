## When to use

Establishing where things stand with a vendor agreement - what is signed, what it commits us to, and what is coming up.

## Find the whole agreement

A vendor relationship is rarely one document: a master agreement, an order form, a data processing addendum, a security schedule, and possibly amendments. Assemble all of them and check the order-of-precedence clause, which decides which one wins where they conflict.

## Extract the dates and the money

- Effective date, initial term, current term.
- Renewal mechanism and the notice deadline - with the actual calendar date the notice must be given by.
- Current committed spend, and the uplift mechanism and cap.
- Any minimum commitment and whether it is being met.

## Check obligations both ways

What we owe them (payment terms, usage limits, notification duties) and what they owe us (SLA, support response, security commitments, audit rights). Then check whether either side is actually complying - an unenforced SLA is a discount nobody claimed.

## Gotchas

- Auto-renewal with a 90-day notice window means the decision date is three months before the date everyone has in mind. Put it in a calendar with a reminder.
- An amendment can change the term without changing the master agreement; check for them specifically.
- Note whether the agreement is with the entity that is actually using the service. Group structures make this wrong more often than expected.
