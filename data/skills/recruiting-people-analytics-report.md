## When to use

Reporting on the workforce - headcount, attrition, hiring, distribution - for people who will make decisions from it.

## Define every metric on the page

Headcount at a point or an average? Does it include contractors? Is attrition voluntary, involuntary or both, and is the denominator the start-of-period headcount or the average? Every one of these choices changes the number materially, and a report that does not state them will be argued with rather than acted on.

## Report attrition as a rate with a cohort view

Annualised rate against the comparable prior period, and separately: voluntary against involuntary, by tenure band, and by function. Attrition concentrated in the first year is a hiring or onboarding problem; attrition concentrated at two years is a progression problem. The single blended number distinguishes neither.

## Be careful with small numbers

Below a threshold - commonly five - a group's figures both mislead and risk identifying individuals. Suppress rather than report, and say that suppression happened rather than leaving a gap.

## Gotchas

- Never report anything that identifies an individual, including by combining two cuts that are each safe alone.
- Distinguish a change in the workforce from a change in how it is recorded; reclassification looks exactly like movement.
- Pair every number with what it would take to change it. A report that only describes is a report that produces a discussion and no action.
