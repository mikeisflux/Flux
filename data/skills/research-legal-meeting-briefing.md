## When to use

Preparing someone for a meeting with a legal dimension - a negotiation, a dispute discussion, a regulator.

## One page, and it is the position

- **The objective** - what a good outcome is, in one sentence.
- **The position** - what we are asserting, and the basis for it.
- **The fallback** - what we can concede, in what order, and what we cannot.
- **Their likely position**, and the strongest version of their argument. Preparing against the weak version is how meetings go badly.
- **The must-not-says** - anything that would prejudice a position or waive privilege.

## Get the facts and the documents in order

A dated chronology with the source document for each entry, and the two or three documents likely to be referred to, tabbed. Being unable to find a clause in the room costs more than being wrong about it.

## Agree who speaks

Especially where legal and commercial people are both present. An unplanned concession from the commercial side in a legal meeting is the most common way a position is lost.

## Gotchas

- Note explicitly whether the meeting is without prejudice, and whether everyone attending knows what that means.
- Do not brief a position that has not been agreed internally; the meeting is not the place to discover a disagreement.
- Record what was said afterwards, promptly, while it is accurate.
