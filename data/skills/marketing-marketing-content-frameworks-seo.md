## When to use

Planning content that has to both rank and be worth reading.

## Start from the query, and from what already ranks

Look at the actual results page for the target query, not at a keyword tool's difficulty score. What is ranking tells you the format the search engine has decided the intent calls for - a comparison, a how-to, a definition, a tool. Publishing a different format is fighting the intent, and format mismatch is the most common reason good content does not rank.

## Structure so it can be understood

- One page per intent. A page trying to rank for two different intents ranks for neither.
- H1 matching the query; H2s matching the sub-questions people actually ask.
- Answer the headline question in the first hundred words. This serves the skimmer and the snippet at once.
- Internal links to and from the related pages, with descriptive anchor text.

## Frameworks that earn their place

- **Problem-agitate-solve** for pages aimed at a felt pain.
- **Comparison table first, prose after** for evaluation queries - the reader wants the table.
- **Step-by-step with a checkable outcome** for how-to.

Pick one and follow it; a piece that switches framework halfway loses the reader.

## Gotchas

- Word count is not a ranking factor. Matching the intent completely is; padding to hit a length actively hurts.
- Update and consolidate before publishing more. Two thin pages on one topic compete with each other.
- Anything written primarily for a crawler reads that way to a person, and people are who decides whether it converts.
