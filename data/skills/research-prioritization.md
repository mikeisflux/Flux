## When to use

More sources than time, and the job is to read the ones that will change the answer.

## Rank by what would move the conclusion

Read in the order that could most change what you currently believe. A source that would confirm the working answer is worth less than one that could refute it, and the instinct runs the other way.

## Judge a source before reading it

- **Primary or secondary?** A filing, a dataset, a first-hand account beats an article about one.
- **Who benefits** from this being believed?
- **When** - and does the thing being described change quickly?
- **Method** - for anything quantitative, is the method stated? An unstated method is an unusable number.
- **Independence** - three articles citing one study are one source.

## Stop deliberately

Set the stopping condition up front: when new sources stop changing the answer, or when the remaining questions cannot be settled by reading. Research without a stopping rule expands to fill whatever time exists.

## Gotchas

- The most accessible sources are the most read and therefore the least differentiating.
- A source that everyone cites is often citing something nobody has read. Follow it back once.
- Record what you rejected and why; that list is part of the finding.
